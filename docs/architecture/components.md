# Components

`base` holds NSGo's built-in unstyled components, after NSH's `nsh-base` (`nsh/docs/dev/components.md`, `dock.md`): they own behavior, state and accessibility and take every style from the app. A component is a file of `base`, or a package under it when it is complex: `base/commitgraph`, `base/datatable`, `base/dock`, `base/plot` and `base/toast`. Read this before changing a component in `base`; [text view](textview.md) covers `base/textview`, and [editor](editor.md) `base/editor` and `base/diff`.

A component whose root is a view of its own takes that view's `OnLayout` and `Tooltip`, as NSH's components take their host's.

Each part a component styles takes Tailwind classes beside its style, as NSH's `class` and `popupClass` do: `Class` with `Style`, `PopupClass` with `PopupStyle`, `Classes` with `Styles`; `classes_test.go` holds every part to it. A part's class goes under its style and under what the component sets on the part itself (a sheet's place, a handle's 1-point line), as classes go under `Style` in `core`, where NSH puts an app's classes over the latter; important classes (`w-[3px]!`) go over both. Parts whose classes follow a state, a table's rows and cells and a toast's phases, take them through `core.View.ClassFunc`.

## Controls

`Button`, `Checkbox`, `Radio` in a `RadioGroup`, `Switch`, `Tab` in `Tabs`, `Collapsible` and `Progress` hold no value: the app passes it and sets it from `OnChange` or `OnPress`, as NSH's controlled props do.

`Invalid func() bool` on buttons, choices, value controls and their groups follows the app's validation through `core.View.Invalid`: `aria-invalid:` classes and `Style.AriaInvalid` update without remounting or disabling the control. Composite fields pass it to their native input, sliders to their adjustable thumbs, and decorative parts through `PartStates.Invalid`. `ColorPicker` shares it with its fields, areas, sliders and eyedropper; a part may also mark itself invalid.

`Checkbox.OnChangeEvent` takes the requested value and its `core.PressEvent`, so an app can implement Shift-press range selection without reaching into the rendered view. When set, it replaces `OnChange`; the app still owns the value. `ui.Checkbox` forwards it, and TSX receives `{ checked, press }`.

`Tabs` and `RadioGroup` are one Tab stop (`base/roving.go`, as NSH's `roving.rs`): the item the focus is on, else the selected tab or checked radio, else the first enabled one is `Focusable`. Their arrows, bound in the keymap only while an item has the focus, focus the item they reach and activate it, swapped right to left, and `Tabs` adds Home and End. The items sort in tree order (`Ref.Before`), since an `Each` may mount one between others. `HitSlop` goes to the view of `Button`, `Checkbox`, `Radio` and `Switch`; `Toggle` and `Link` also take `HitSlopFunc`, which follows what it reads.

`Toolbar` and `ToggleGroup` are bars (`barItem`): groups of the same roving focus whose `Button`s, `Toggle`s and `Link`s join them, in a `ToolbarGroup` or a `ToggleGroup` inside a toolbar too, disabled with the bar. An arrow key only moves the focus, along a toolbar's `Axis`, and a toolbar's Tab comes back to the item it left where a toggle group's enters at the first pressed toggle. A `Menu`'s or `Popover`'s trigger is one of a bar's items, with what the app puts in it no item of its own, and with one of a toolbar's menus open the pointer over another's trigger moves there, as a menu bar does.

`Slider` and `NumberInput` share `numeric.go`, NSH's rules of a number in a range with a step and of one typed as text. A drag's value shows at once until the drag ends, without waiting for the app's `Value` to come back, and assistive technology hears it at the end unless a screen reader runs, so a drag sends no props item per move unless the slider's range changes. `base.Slider` reads `Min`, `Max` and `Step` reactively; `ui.Slider` exposes their `…Func` twins beside static fields and rebuilds only its marks when they change, keeping focus and an active drag in place. Slider thumbs bind `LabelFunc` and their range-specific label functions without changing the focus or drag state. Number and code inputs bind `LabelFunc` on their original field. A number input follows the signals its `Format` reads outside editing, deferring that text update during a focused draft. A slider's `Value` is `SliderValue`, one number or a range, and a number input's `*float64`, nil for an empty field, the shapes TSX's `number | [number, number]` and `number | null` read as. NSH lets go of a drag's value once a TSX app has answered, a turn later; handlers run synchronously here, so the release does.

`OtpInput` is one transparent native field over cells that only show the code, which keeps typing, input methods, paste and the system's code autofill the platform's. Edits are read from what the field reports and apply to the code as typed, so a controlled `Value` a few keys behind is no conflict (`otpEchoes`).

`Accordion`'s and `Collapsible`'s `Reveal` animate the commit that opens or closes them with a critically damped layout spring, and the content, in a `Holder`, comes back as it was. `Avatar` lays its image out transparent over the fallback, so loading changes those two parts and nothing else, with an image node per source so a late event of the old one is not the new one's. `Pagination` makes its row once, a place for each item it can hold, so a page change makes no view. `Tree` flattens the items once per change of them and looks rows up by bisection; a move of the selection wakes the rows it leaves and reaches, spread over 32 signals (`treeFollow`). `Carousel` is a native scroll view snapping to where its slides report they are laid out, a looping one with a period of runway each side and each slide drawn in the period nearest the viewport's center by an animated translation.

`Scrollbar` watches its target with `Scope.WatchScroll` and scrolls it with `Ref.ScrollTo`; its built-in form is [scrolling](scrolling.md#bars-of-the-apps)'s.

`Tabs`' indicator is a view that follows the selected tab (`View.Follow`, `core/follow.go`, as NSH's `follow.rs`). After each layout the core spans the target's frame along the parent's main axis, inside the follower's margins there, and leaves the cross axis to the follower's layout, so a change of tab is one frame item with `FollowTransition`, and a layout that moves the tabs moves it in the same batch.

`Input` is the core's text field under a name in `base`, as NSH's `nsh/base` names its core's, so apps take it with the other controls; `NavPage` is `core.Screen` for a `Nav`'s pages the same way.

- A control's parts (a checkbox's indicator, a switch's track and thumb) follow its states through `View.PartStates`, which styles a view by states it does not report, so assistive technology hears them from the control alone. Content the app puts inside follows them with a `GroupStyle` under the control's `Style.Group`.
- A radio reports checked and selected both, as `gpui-base`'s does, since screen readers read one or the other.
- `Collapsible` makes its content the first time it opens and keeps it, hidden, while closed.
- `Progress` is one accessible element with a range from 0 to 100, and none while indeterminate.

| NSH | NSGo | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Non-field controls omit invalid state | Controls expose `Invalid` | Validation styles must work on buttons and choices as well as text fields (#458) |
| A slot's element in a view of its own | The element in the component's row | Go's `core.Node` needs no holder, so `NumberInput`'s frames count from its row |
| `Scrollbar`'s `axis`, vertical by default | `Horizontal` | Go's zero value is the default |
| `Collapsible` and `AccordionPanel` keep their `reveal` as it changes | `Reveal` read as a `Collapsible` is made | Its two forms are different views |
| Items ordered by the core's `watch_order` | `Ref.Before` as they are read | One way to order, as `Tabs` has |
| `Checkbox` reports only the next value | Optional `OnChangeEvent` also reports the press | Apps need modifiers for range selection, while existing `OnChange` calls keep their contract |

## What only NSGo has

`TagInput`, `Rating`, `HoldToConfirm`, `ImageCompare`, `Stepper`, `Sidebar` and `FormSection` have no `base` form yet and live whole in `ui`, and the gallery marks them on their cards. `Sidebar` folds a `Collapsible` section with `base.Collapsible`, under an arrow that takes no room and shows while the pointer or the focus is on the title, and takes the keys after a press, as `List` does, since a click leaves the focus where it was. `FormSection` is an inset grouped list, as iOS Settings has: its rows, `FormRow` and `FormField`, sit on one rounded card with `CardStyle` and `CardStyleFunc` for its surface. A `FormRow` takes `Below` in its text column, a `TitleMaxLines` limit and an explicit `MinHeight`; `core.RoleRadio` delegates controlled checked state and group keyboard behavior to `base.Radio`. The hairline below a row starts at its title, using the width of the icon tile or the slot its `Leading` node occupies, unless `DividerInset` overrides that child’s inset; zero is full width and a false override preserves the automatic alignment. A row from a `core.Each` among them takes its own hairline, and the card's last view hides its one (`last:hidden`), so the hairlines stay right as rows come, go and move and as an `Each` empties. `base.List` is NSGo's own: NSH leaves a flat list of choices to each app's `<List>`, so it holds what such a list needs over `core.List` (the selection, the keys, typeahead and the listbox's roles), and `ui.List` is only its look. `List.OnSelectionChange` hears the rows a press, a key or typeahead chooses, never the app's own writes to the `Selection`, as `Tree.OnSelectedChange` and `DataTable.OnSelectionChange` hear only the user's choices. `Selection` is state rather than a component: the rows a `List` chooses by press and keyboard, a multiple one with Shift and Mod, and `ui.Tree`'s the same way. `base.Tree` takes NSH's props and `Row`, a cell that follows its item, where `ui.Tree` has its chevron and a surface taking the row's presses: there a press only selects, a double press or the chevron expands, and a collapsed branch holds a child no row shows, so its children are read only as it expands. `Slider`, `NumberInput`, `Carousel` and `Progress` likewise take what the look draws around them: marks, a scrub handle, a commit as the field is left, the content's offset, the thumb's ref for a slider drawn by a shader.

## Popups

Popovers, tooltips, menus, context menus, dialogs and sheets share NSH's contract (`popup.go`): `Open` makes them controlled, `OnOpenChange` reports each request with its `Reason`, `OnCancel` every attempt to close, and `OnOpen` and `OnClose` follow what is shown, after a sheet's motion. `OnOpen` comes after the commit that made the content (`Scope.Post`), so its refs reach their views and a `Focus` there moves the keyboard focus in; a popup gone before then reports neither. A popover or a desktop menu holding the focus gives it back to its trigger as it starts to close, not once its exit ends (`focusBack`), so a key pressed while it goes is the trigger's, unless a press outside took the focus elsewhere. A popover's panel follows its trigger in the Tab order: the core gives each shown dialog overlay the native view before it where the app put it as `ViewProps.TabAfter`, which the test driver orders Tab by, while AppKit's key view loop and the browser's DOM, which order it themselves, leave the panel at the end, as NSH's do.

Menus and dropdowns take the form their `Presentation` names, the same on every device, where NSH's swap it on touch screens (decided by the user, 2026-11-12); `base.OnSheet` tells a styled item which it is in:

| | `PresentPopup` (the zero value) | `PresentSheet` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Menu` | A popup at its trigger; submenus cascade beside their item, on hover or press | A sheet at the bottom over `BackdropStyle`; a submenu's items replace its menu's, below its `Back` row, and Escape or back return a level |
| `ContextMenu` | A popup at the pointer (`AnchorBounds` at the click) | The menu's sheet |
| `Select`, `Combobox`, `DatePicker` | A popup at the trigger, as wide as it or at least that wide, never past the room on its side | A sheet at the bottom over a backdrop, below the status bar at most; a search field takes the keyboard only when pressed |

A `ContextMenu` opens on a secondary click, and on touch screens after a finger rests 400 ms, watched with `OnTouch` so the trigger's own presses and scroll views go on; 10 points of movement cancel. A dropdown closed by a press outside leaves the focus where it went, any other close on a desktop giving it back to the trigger. The other popups follow what the device can do:

| | Desktops | Touch screens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Popover` | A press outside closes it and goes on to what is under it | A transparent backdrop overlay takes the press outside |
| `Tooltip` | After the pointer rests half a second, or at once as the keyboard moves the focus to the trigger | As the keyboard moves the focus to the trigger, or when the app opens it |
| `HoverCard` | Opens after the pointer rests `OpenDelay` (600 ms) or the focus comes to the trigger, stays while either is on the trigger or the card, closes `CloseDelay` (300 ms) after both left; Escape and a press outside close it at once | A press on the trigger opens and closes it, a press outside closes it and goes on |

Items reach their menu through a context key (`menuContext`), so the app arranges them freely. A desktop popup's items and submenus' triggers join their level (`menukeys.go`), whose keys bind while it is open, never on its way out: Down and Up around the ends, Home and End, Page Down and Page Up to the last and first item in view and a view on from there, stopping at the ends, Right into a submenu with the focus on its first item and Left or Escape back to its trigger. They go on from the item the focus is on or the pointer came over last, else from an end, and a menu a key opened (`PressEvent.Keyboard`) focuses its first item. Each level's items sit in a scroll view inside its popup, with the popup style's padding and gap moved onto them, and `Overlay.HoldToRoom` holds the popup to the room on the side the core picks at its natural height. A virtualized list of items in a menu, NSH's `MenuList`, is not ported: its rows past the window take no keys. A `MenuItem` with `Checked` is a `RoleMenuItemCheckbox`, or the `Role` it gives; its `Indicator` is a part around the app's mark that takes the item's checked and disabled states. A sheet's hidden levels are out of layout and their content out of the tree, so screen readers and tests see one level.

- `Dialog.OnConfirm` and `Sheet.OnConfirm` are a default action: the layer binds Return (`cx.OnKey`, `KeyAuto`) from the owner that shows only it, so a modal over it shadows the binding, a focused button, menu row or text input inside keeps Return, and content bindings made inside win as newer ones. Return runs the action through the popup's own close request, which runs nothing once the popup is closed, so a key repeated as it goes runs it once. `ui.AlertDialog` sets `RoleAlertDialog` in either presentation, focuses its first `Default` action through `base.Button.AutoFocus`, runs its first `AlertCancel` action before reporting an Escape or back close, and closes itself once any action ran until `Open` turns false and true again, as `base.Dialog` does after Escape. An unmarked alert keeps its previous keyboard behavior.
- `Sheet` and a phone's menus are overlays, not modals: `sheet.Motion` (`internal/sheet`) ports NSH's `SheetMotion`. The panel's translation and the backdrop's opacity are animated nodes of one `fade` value and the panel's measured size, so they run on the platform's frame clock; the parts leave the tree when the motion out ends, or after its time where no end is reported.
- With `CloseOnSwipe` a bottom sheet's `PanValues` move a drag value; a release past 30% of its height or 800 points a second asks to close (`ReasonSwipe`), else it comes back. On iOS the panel's pan hands off from the scroll view the drag starts in (`View.PanScrollHandoff`): the drag scrolls it back to its top first, and what goes on past that moves the sheet while the scroll view stays pinned there. Elsewhere the scroll view keeps the drag.
- `View.Tooltip` is the short form: the core shows the node above the view, 4 points away, after half a second of hover on desktops, and at once while the focus the keyboard moved is on the view or inside it, until it leaves or Escape is pressed. Whether the keyboard moved the focus is decided as it moves, by the rule `focus-visible:` follows (`keyboardMovesFocus`), without its exception for inputs that edit, so a press or a tap that focuses the trigger leaves it to the pointer. A `Tooltip` given there is the hint itself, as NSH's: it shows against the view when its own open state says, the core telling it of the pointer and the focus (`core.HintTooltip`, `HintChange`), so an app opens it on touch screens too; `base.Tooltip` with a `Trigger` is such a view around it. Tooltips never take the pointer (`Overlay.PointerEvents`); their content stays mounted with `DisplayNone` while closed, so it can describe the trigger before focus, and their bubbles are hidden from assistive technology ([accessibility](interaction.md#accessibility)).
- An overlay popup (`Popover`, `Tooltip`, and a `Menu`'s or `ContextMenu`'s on desktops) comes and goes through `popupMotion` (`popup.go`), as Base UI's `data-starting-style` and `data-ending-style`: `StartingStyle` in its first frame (the next display frame, or 50 ms), so the app's `Transition` runs the entry, and `EndingStyle` as it closes, leaving the tree (a tooltip stays mounted, hidden in its starting part, so showing it again runs the same entry) on the overlay's `OnTransitionEnd`; `OnClose` follows that. Both merge over the popup's style and their classes are important, as a toast's. Without an ending part, or under reduced motion, it goes at once. A desktop menu's submenus take its phases, and closing a menu closes each with its own exit. NSH waits out a transition by asking the core its length; `OnTransitionEnd` here says when it ended.
- `Dialog` stays a `core.Modal` the platform presents. Given `StartingStyle` and `EndingStyle`, or their classes, it comes and goes through `popupMotion` too, with the backdrop's phases following: the modal stays until the popup's `OnTransitionEnd`, and what is going takes no presses. `ui.Dialog` does on desktops: a dim that fades and a surface that pops in and fades out, above the middle of the window. `AlertDialog` is the system's alert, or with `AlertPresentActionSheet` its action sheet: `UIAlertController`'s (a popover anchored at the bottom on iPad, with an unshown cancel action for a tap outside), a list dialog on Android, stacked buttons with the cancel one last on macOS, the web and Windows.

Popups are placed against what their trigger holds (`Overlay.AnchorContent`), the app's element inside the view a column stretches, as NSH places them against the element itself, and a press anywhere in that view is still no press outside.

## Pickers

`Select`, `Combobox` and `SuggestList` share `listbox.go`, as NSH's `listbox.rs`, the state machine `gpui-base` leaves to a styled layer: `listShown`, the rows for a query, with a query that holds the last one searching only its matches; the highlight's moves, typeahead, and where the highlight goes as the items change; rows of a `List` with `ListViewportParent`, so a popup is as tall as its rows up to its room and a list of thousands makes the rows in view. A move of the highlight wakes the two rows it leaves and reaches, spread over 32 signals. `RenderItem` gets what an item is, never its state: the row's `Selected` and `Focused` follow through `group-*` styles, so nothing draws again. A desktop chooses as the button goes down, which keeps a search field's focus. `dropdown` (`popup.go`) is the popup or sheet `Select`, `Combobox` and `DatePicker` open; a `Select`'s lays its selected row over the trigger (`Overlay.Cover`, shadcn/ui's `item-aligned`). `SuggestList` puts the list in an overlay at a `core.Suggest`'s caret instead, passes it no query, so it shows the app's rows as they come, and binds the keys beside the field while no input method composes ([mention completion](mention-completion.md)).

`Combobox` keeps the value as the user last left it, ahead of an app's that follows, so toggles in a row start from it and `OnConfirm` reports it on every close. `Command` is the same list in the page: its own state (`palette`, `command.go`) with separators, and a highlight that keeps its item as items change and goes to the first match as the query does. It departs from NSH's for a launcher's search: every word of a query must match, and exact labels rank before labels it starts, labels holding it and keywords (`FilterCommands`, which `Filter` replaces); a group gathers wherever its items are, showing where its best match ranks; items carry a description, an icon and leading and trailing content for the app's row; `Pending` and `Error` take the rows' place and suppress choosing; `ExpandOnSearch` holds the rows back until there is a query; and `InputSource` is its search field's, as `core.Input`'s, so a palette kept mounted selects it each time it takes the focus. Its keys unbind while an input method composes. `ui.CommandDialog` hosts `ui.Command` in a dialog.

`Calendar` is 42 cells made once per month, six weeks for every month so that it keeps its size, a turn of the months changing what they show, with one Tab stop whose keys turn the months; `DatePicker` holds it, with presets and, for one date with `TimePrecision`, a `TimeField`, which is one transparent native field over segments, as `OtpInput`. `ColorPicker` keeps a color in RGB, HSL, HSB and OKLCH (`colormodel.go`) so a gray keeps its hue, and its parts reach it through a context key. A color the app sets back that the picker reported, as itself or as its text read again, changes nothing, so rounding moves no thumb. A channel's `ColorField` is a `NumberInput` that takes what is typed at once, with `Live`, ending the change as the field is left, and `Number` hands it to the look. `ColorChannels` shows the three of the notation `ColorNotationMenu` chooses, and `ColorCopyMenu` copies the color; their `Draw` is the look's menu. `ColorSwatch` only shows a color, where NSH's picks a preset: the user dropped presets. `ColorEyeDropper` enables where `Env.CanPickScreenColor` is true (macOS, Windows, Linux with the desktop Screenshot portal, and browsers with `EyeDropper`), and `ui.ColorPicker` shows it there.

`ui`'s forms draw these parts and hold no behavior of their own: `SearchField` and `Field` hand a form the input to draw, as `ui.Input`, and `ItemStyleFunc`, `ItemStyleOf` and the like follow a theme. `ui.Dialog` and its `ui.AlertDialog` wrapper merge the app's `Style`, then `StyleFunc`, inside the base panel's existing style binding, for both centered dialogs and sheets; signal changes restyle the panel without rebuilding its content. `ui.Select` and `ui.Combobox` value a list's items by their index among `Options`, so a value may be any comparable type. Their words come from `base/locales`.

### Emoji pickers

`base.EmojiPicker` searches every Unicode emoji by its CLDR names, keywords and shortcodes in the app's languages, using a virtualized `core.List` of category headers and `Columns`-wide rows (8 by default), a category tab bar (`RoleTabList`), an optional `Recent` section, keyboard navigation (arrows with RTL mirroring, Page Up/Down, Home/End, Return/Space, Escape clearing the query then calling `OnCancel`, suspended during IME composition), and controlled or uncontrolled `SkinTone` selection. All default labels, placeholders, empty messages, category names and skin tone names route through `base/locales`.

- The catalog and 28 locale tables live in `base/internal/emoji/emoji.bin` (generated from pinned `emojibase-data` via `go generate ./base/internal/emoji`), each locale deflated separately and inflated on demand. `base/internal/emoji` has no package `init` or eager decoding, so Go's linker strips it completely from apps that never reach `EmojiPicker` or its lookup helpers (`SearchEmojis`, `EmojisInCategory`, `EmojiByString`), verified by `TestTextViewAppLinks`.
- By default, search follows `i18n.Language(cx)` and `cx.Env().Locale()` with English fallback (primary language matches rank ahead of fallback), or explicit `Languages`/`LanguagesFunc`.
- Category tabs use `Tabs` and skin tone radios share its roving-focus infrastructure; category props scroll the virtual grid, and changing results refreshes the preview even when its index stays the same. Public lookup metadata owns its slices, and applying a tone selects an available equal-tone variant, including pairs with mixed-tone variants in the catalog. `EmojiPickerRef` owns query access for the styled clear button so behavior stays in `base`.
- `ui.EmojiPicker` styles `base.EmojiPicker` in NSGo's theme tokens (320 × 360 pt surface, search field with clear button, Lucide category icons, highlighted emoji preview and skin tone swatches) with no second state implementation.

| NSH | NSGo | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A trigger's role and presses bound on the app's element | A view around it, `Class` and `Style` the view's | Go's `core.Node` cannot be changed from outside, so frames count from the view |
| `value` as `null` or absent, `Date` an enum | `Value func() *string`, `DateValue` with `Range` and zero times unset | Go's zero values |
| `searchable`, `filterable`, `separator` | `Command.NoSearch`, `NoFilter`, `NoSeparator` | Go's zero value is the default |
| Menus and dropdowns as sheets on touch screens, a dock compact below 600 points | `Presentation` and `dock.Area.Compact`, a popup and docked panels unless the app asks | `base` changes nothing by device ([principles](../../AGENTS.md#principles)) |
| A `DateMatcher` of days, bounds or a function | `DisabledMatcher func(time.Time) bool`, made by `DaysOfWeek`, `DatesOutside` and `DatesBetween` | A Go function says all of them; TSX's forms read as NSH's |
| A JS matcher asked in the next turn | Asked as a day is read, once per date | Handlers run synchronously here |
| `HoverCard` delays of 0 and `Infinity` | Zero is the default, negative at once, `HoverNever` for good | Go's zero value is the default; TSX's read as NSH's |

## Toasts

`Toast`, `toast.Stack` and `toast.Manager` are NSH's, which are `gpui-base`'s three pieces with its names; `ui`'s `Toaster` and `Toasts` show them from anywhere, at any of eight placements, with the stack turned around (`Stack.Reverse`), the swipe's direction and a limit, and the gallery's are those. NSH removed the system toast for them, and so `Scope.ShowToast` is gone.

- `ToastManager[K, V]` is plain data with no timer, generic over the app's ids and values: the app calls `Advance` on its own timer while toasts show, with `Scope.Now`, so a test's manual clock moves it. Its zero value has Sonner's motion, and `Push` takes the timeout directly, where NSH has `new(motion)` and `ToastOptions`.
- `ToastStack[T, K]` takes the toasts as `Toasts`, `Key` and `Toast`, an `Each`, in place of NSH's children each wrapped as an item. Each item is an absolute view placed in the layout at its distance from the edge; `TranslateY` plays the way there from where it was, with `ScaleX` and `Opacity`, by critically damped springs on the frame clock. The items' measured heights give the distances and the stack's height, so its hover area follows. Distances count from the line that stays put as the height changes, the anchored edge or a center anchor's middle, so `Reverse` and center anchors move nothing but what changed. An ending `Toast` tells its item (`leavingKey`) and takes no room from then on: it stays where it shows while the others close up, where NSH's toasts hold their place until they are removed. A finger down (observed with `OnTouch`) expands it as hover does, and a zero-size overlay hears Escape and back while it holds toasts.
- `Toast` is the alert role, announces its `Label` as it mounts and its `LabelFunc` again each time it changes, as a toast that replaces one of the same ID does, and takes a swipe through the touch responder, claimed once the pointer moved 10 points along `SwipeDirection`. `StartingStyle` applies until the next display frame (or 50 ms), so the app's `Transition` runs the entry; `EndingStyle` and `SwipingStyle` merge over its style, and the phases' classes are made important, as NSH's are. The swipe's translation goes on a view around the root, whose `Transition` backends would otherwise drop; while ending, the root takes no presses. `ui.ToastContent` can omit its close button with `NoClose`, append a `Trailing` node, and make the whole toast a button with `OnPress`; `Toasts.NoClose` omits close buttons for the stack. The styled surface combines the theme, the stack's `Style` and `StyleFunc`, then the toast's `Style` and `StyleFunc` in the base root's style binding. Starting, ending and swipe styles apply at their usual phases.

## Charts

`base/plot` ports `nsh/base/plot` (`nsh/website/elements/plot.md`). A `Plot` holds the rows' columns, its scales and the hover; its marks (`Line`, `Area`, `Bar`, `Rule`, `Dot`, `Cell`, `Grid`, `Pie`, `Arc`, `RadialLine`, `RadialGrid`, `Sankey`, `Treemap`), `Axis` and `Label` draw what it holds. Each mark is a canvas filling the plot and the bleed around it its strokes and curves need, a canvas per slice, node, ribbon or class where each paints with styles of its own; it records once when its points, the plot's size or scales change, and paints with the paint properties it inherits ([canvas](canvas.md#view-paints)).

- Marks reach their plot through a context key and add themselves to it: their extent makes the domain of the axis their values go along when the scale gives none, and each `Line` and `Area` has a hover dot. Marks that find rows themselves (a slice, a dot, a cell, a node) add a hit test, and with one the plot follows what it finds rather than the row nearest along the axis.
- A band scale gives each row or category a band. Bars that share a `Stack` pile up from its first bar's base; the others stand side by side in a band.
- `ColorScale` colors categories (`OrdinalColors`, d3's implicit domain) and numbers along a ramp mixed in OKLab (`SequentialColors`, `DivergingColors`), as a mark's paint where nothing sets one.
- `Y2Scale` is a second y scale. While both y domains are automatic, they widen to the same number of nice steps, so one `Grid` serves both axes. A y `Axis`'s outside labels keep the scale's range inside the plot through insets; a label not laid out yet asks for the tallest one's room, as the steps depend on that room and the labels on the steps, which otherwise never settles (a departure from NSH).
- The hover never records: the pointer moves animated values and lands in the same frame, a step of the keys or a screen reader glides on `Motion.Pointer`, the parts fade on `Enter` and `Exit`, and the tooltip is clamped inside the plot by animated math from the plot's size and its own, as NSH's.
- The keys bind while the plot has the focus, Up and Down only over a grid and Escape only while a row is hovered, so they take nothing from the app around it.
- The appear's time runs linearly and each mark eases its own slice: lines clip to an edge `Bind` animates and record once more without the clip when it ends, bars and cells grow, a pie sweeps. `AppearScope` remembers the generations that finished, so a list's row made again shows whole.
- `Motion.Update` moves a linear domain (`tween`): places on the new scale show at `scale*at+offset`, two nodes the domain's ends drive, which paths record through (`Drawing.Transform` bound, undone before the stroke) and bound views derive from, so no mark records per frame. Every mark has a pose of its own (`moves`) and draws each number as a line from the old pose to the new one; a change while one runs reads how far it had gone and moves on from there. `Bar`'s, `Rule`'s, `Line`'s, `Area`'s and `Label`'s poses are their rows' values (`valuePose`), placed on the new scale and drawn through the domain's transform, so a value and its domain move together from the old pixel to the new one; rows match by their own x where they have one, so a window that slides keeps its points, else by index when their number stays. NSH moves only these marks' domains, so new values jump and then glide with the scale (a departure from NSH). While a domain moves, an axis's labels fade out past the plot's ends. A cell whose color changes draws its old color at what is left of the move and adds the new one (`CompositeLighter`) at what has gone, so a translucent ramp keeps its opacity, where one over the other would thicken. A slice's corners are not linear in its angles, so its numbers are sampled along the motion.
- A moving rectangle with corners records its own arcs, each radius fitted to the rectangle by animated math, as no op takes a rounded rectangle's arguments by slot.
- A `Sankey` lays out d3-sankey's algorithm with gpui-base's changes, a `Treemap` d3's squarified tiling over `stratify`.
- `Area`'s, `Bar`'s, `RadialLine`'s and `Sankey`'s background shows inside the shape through `FillBackgroundFromView`.

## Tables

`Table` and its parts (`TableHeader`, `TableBody`, `TableFooter`, `TableRow`, `TableHead`, `TableCell`, `TableCaption`) give the app's few rows a table's roles, `RoleTable` around `RoleRowGroup`, `RoleRow`, `RoleColumnHeader` and `RoleCell`, which each backend maps to its accessibility: AppKit's and WebKit's table roles, ARIA's, Android's role descriptions, and VoiceOver's containers with headers on iOS.

`DataTable` ports NSH's (`nsh/docs/dev/data-table.md`): the pure parts in `datatable/layout.go` (the scaled axis past 3 000 000 points, the columns' prefix sums, the selection's keys, the row window and slots), the controller in `datatable.go` and the layers in `datatable/view.go`.

- The body takes no pointer and places the rows and columns in view from the offsets the scroll events report, on whole device pixels. Over it a vertical `Scroll` of a horizontal one of an empty box is the hit layer the platform scrolls, drawing nothing but its bar, the platform's or the app's (`Bar`, `Scope.SetScrollbars`), which so lies over a striped or selected row; the header above it is interactive. Desktops get a horizontal scroll bar band over the body's bottom, following the table's scroll view without echoing its moves back, since the inner view's own bar lies at the end of a content as tall as all the rows.
- Rows and scrolling columns are slots made once (`slotItem`): a scroll gives slots other rows, so no view is made while scrolling. The rows made are as many as the viewport shows at any offset and half a viewport each way, as many more on one side as the table's end cuts from the other (`rowWindow`), so the window keeps its length and moves with every row: counted from the rows in view, which a scroll takes one past and back at each edge, a slot was hidden and shown again, its cells' texts and frames with it, and a frame crossing rows sent 59 items rather than 33. The columns' window is NSH's (`window.Rows`, reaching one column and three), and only the cells of the columns in view and one each way read their texts; the others keep theirs, hidden. Rows are placed relative to a chunk of 1024, columns of 256, so places stay small at any offset.
- One effect sets the windows and chunk origins from the scroll offsets into signals: a scroll reaches the region boxes and the rows it brings, not every cell ([reactive graph](core.md#reactive-graph)). The region boxes are sized in points, so the rows moving inside one lay out that box alone ([commit](core.md#commit)).
- The regions also move with the scroll views on the main thread, in the frame the content does: each is translated by how far the scroll views went since the commit that placed it (`tableFollow`: the scroll views' `OffsetValues` through the scaled axis, less a base each commit sets with the places it lays out). The cells then keep up with a scroll however long a commit takes, and at rest the translation is what rounding left, so frames, hit testing and accessibility stay where the cells show. The translation stops at the rows made and the columns shown: a jump past them, a scroll bar dragged across a million rows, waits for the commit that brings their cells rather than showing none.
- The columns are in three groups in that order (`tableColumns`, `columnSpec.group`): fixed at the left, scrolling, and fixed at the right (`FixedRight`, NSH's `fixed: "right"`), each in a region of its own between which no column moves; the scrolling region's view is the table's width less both fixed groups, and the right group's region sits at its end, with its own edge line.
- A cell's text fills it and aligns with `TextAlign`, so a new text keeps its frame. A cell restyles for `PositiveStyle` and `NegativeStyle` only when its sign changes.
- What `RenderCell` draws takes no pointer, so a tooltip there could never open: `CellTooltip` gives a body cell its tooltip instead (`cellTooltip`, a `HintTooltip` on the cell's view), opened by the hit layer, which waits half a second on a cell the pointer rests on (`tipAt`, `tip`) and closes it as the pointer leaves the cell or the table or presses. Its text is also the cell's accessibility description, and `Styles.CellTooltip` gives the tooltip its look, the table having none of its own.
- Cells take presses and context menus from assistive technology and tests; a pointer reaches the hit layer. A title's drag moves its column unless it starts in the resize handle's band or a resize is under way.
- `MultiSelect` makes the selection a sorted set of rows (`tableState.selectedRows`), and cells and columns stop selecting; `tableSel` then only marks the keyboard's row. `SelectedRows` controls that set when supplied, `OnSelectedRowsChange` reports user changes, and each row in it is selected to accessibility. Plain presses replace the set, Command or Control presses toggle a row, Shift presses select the range from the anchor, and Shifted arrows, page keys and Home/End extend it. A press moves the platform's focus to the hit layer, so the table's keys stop when another control takes the keyboard.

| --- | --- | --- |
| `data`, `dataStart`, `rowCount` and objects by key | `RowCount` and `Text(row, column)`, or `Data` of typed columns (`NewRows`, NSH's `DataTableRows`) by key from `DataStart` | Go reads its own data; the table asks for the cells it shows |
| A column's `resizable`, `movable` and `selectable` over the table's | `NoResize`, `NoMove`, `NoSelect` | Go's zero value is the default; a column can only opt out |

## Commit graph

`base/commitgraph` draws a Git history's revision graph, after Onegit's All Commits page; NSH has none. `Layout` is pure: it turns commits listed with each after its children into a `Row` each, the lines crossing it by column. A new line is numbered by the column it opens in, which gives it its color. Open lines stop at `MaxLines` (64): a commit past them keeps its row without a node or a merge's later parents. `Fold` says which commits a collapsed merge hides: those only its later parents reach. A commit something else shown descends from stays.

- `History` is the state: which merges are collapsed, and a memo of the shown commits' rows, which folded parents leave. It moves a selection a merge hides to that merge.
- `Graph` is one row's canvas, as wide as that row's columns. It fills lines, rings and discs in the app's `Color`, the text's when nil, and cuts rings' holes and chevrons out with `CompositeDestinationOut`, so a selected row shows through them without the graph knowing its background. Right to left it counts columns from the right, as the canvas does not mirror.
- A merge's button is a `base.Button` over the canvas, for its label and Expanded state; it sits out of the Tab stops, as a tree's chevron does.
- A row's lines meet the next row's only with no gap or vertical padding between them, which the app's list must keep.

`ui/commitgraph.View` is `ui.List` with `Graph`, refs, subject, author, hash and date, in Onegit's palette. It keeps no second state: its `History` is `base/commitgraph`'s, and only the columns are its own.

## Camera

`base/camera` is a plane the user pans and zooms; NSH has none. The app lays its content out at the content's own size and the view shows it through a camera, fitted and centered until the user moves it, so one plane can hold screens, diagrams or anything else side by side, with the app's tools in `Children` over it.

- The camera moves on the platform's frame clock: its place and scale are animated values, a drag pans by `PanValues` and a finger let go glides. `OnWheel` zooms around the pointer for a wheel, Control or Command held, and pans for a touchpad's two fingers or a sideways wheel; a pinch zooms around its `Position`, and a double press the app does not take zooms in, or out with Shift.
- A layer around the plane takes its pans, pinches and wheel and hands presses and hovers to the app at points of the content (`OnPress`, `OnHover`), as `flow.State.Press` does for a diagram. Interactive content takes its own pointer input through the camera's transform.
- `State.Zoom` and `ToView` follow where the camera is going, for marks the app keeps the same size on screen at any zoom.

## Flow diagram

`base/flow` is NSGo's flow diagram; NSH has none. `layout` is pure: a layered (Sugiyama) drawing that breaks cycles, ranks nodes by longest path and then toward their neighbours' median, orders ranks by barycenters to cut crossings, places nodes by isotonic regression and routes edges through dummies as orthogonal lines on tracks of their own. Bends are kept few: a run of an edge that leaves or reaches a node settles on its middle where its corridors allow, else straight off it rather than bending, and edges of both styles reaching a node's shared side from either hand join it on one track.

- `View` has no camera: it lays out at the size its layout takes, so an app puts it on a `camera.View`, several on one if it likes, and composes its own tools around them. The camera's presses and hovers reach it through `State.Press` and `State.Hover`, and `Camera` follows the focused node.
- It lays out again when the data, the direction or a node's measured size changes (`OnLayout` of its box). A change of topology moves nodes on springs for 600 ms after it; sizes settling outside that place them at once.
- Nodes move on the platform's frame clock: each node's place is an animated value, and the edges' canvas binds every point of a route to its ends' places while they move (`route.at`), each point following the end it is nearer along the route, so no commit runs per frame. At rest the drawing binds nothing.
- Dashes are the canvas's own, a piece per straight run and bend, each piece's offset anchored to the plane, so edges sharing a track dash in step.
- Nodes and their interactive content take pointer input where the camera draws them, as well as keyboard and assistive technology's presses.
- It reads no theme: a `Look`'s zero colors, the selection ring and the empty text take the text color the view has. The edges' canvas paints them from its view (`FillFromView`, `StrokeFromView` with `PaintCurrentColor`, faded by the drawing's alpha), since a color recorded into a drawing cannot be `CurrentColor`, and a label with no background erases the edges under it (`CompositeDestinationOut`), so it reads on whatever is behind.

`ui/flow.Diagram` is `View` in the theme's colors, the accent's selection ring and focus ring, and `Card`, a node's default face, its border a `CardVariant`. `ui/flow.View` puts a `Diagram` on a camera of its own with an overlay in its corner, the chart TSX registers as `Flow`.

| Common approach | NSGo | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The view redraws edges and moves nodes each frame | Animated values and bound canvas arguments | The engine sends what animates once ([boundaries](../../ARCHITECTURE.md#boundaries)) |
| A diagram with its own camera | A camera of the app's, around any content | Several diagrams and other content share one plane and the app's tools |
| Hand-made dashes along flattened bends | The canvas's dashes, a piece at a time | Fewer points to bind while nodes move |
| A pointing hand over nodes | The platform's arrow | Pressables keep it ([components](../../AGENTS.md#components)) |
| `Card`'s `Border` color | `CardVariant` | `ui` takes color from variants |

## Formulas

`base/formula` typesets TeX and Typst math with [Untie](https://github.com/caelyreth/untie) and draws it as a `core.Canvas` of filled shapes; NSH has none, and MyGo's `contrib/mygo` adapter of Untie is the reference. Its font is New Computer Modern Math Book (GUST Font License, that is LPPL 1.3c: `fonts/LICENSE`, `NOTICE`), embedded unmodified, which a test pins by its digest; `NewRenderer` takes an app's fonts.

- A formula is laid out once at 100 points and kept as em-unit paths in runs of one paint, 256 layouts a `Renderer` (`TestLayoutScalesLinearly` holds layout linear in the size). The canvas is sized by `Canvas.Em` in ems of the font it inherits, so it follows the text size, the user's setting and a text's own limits on it, and its baseline sits on its line's through the attachment's `Descent` ([text](text.md#text-and-drawing)).
- Its box is Untie's `Bounds`, with the ink past the box: canvases clip on the web, a `<canvas>` element, and on Linux, an image surface of the view's size ([canvas](canvas.md)), so an inline formula has its overflow's gap on each side rather than lose ink.
- Rules retain Untie's snapping axis separately from glyph paths. At drawing time `PixelGrid` aligns thin rules' minor axis to whole device pixels using the final em scale and environment pixel ratio; glyph outlines, rule lengths and cached layout dimensions stay unchanged on every backend.
- Ink fills from the view's text color (`FillFromView`), which a theme or appearance change resends without drawing again; colors the formula names stay, and errors take `ErrorStyle`'s color. A formula past Untie's limits, or whose fonts did not load, shows its source in `ErrorClass` and `ErrorStyle`.
- `SourceFunc` relays a growing formula's current layout. `Streaming` or `StreamingFunc` hides recoverable error and placeholder ink until the app finishes receiving source, preserving valid ink and authored colors. Clearing it shows remaining errors without rebuilding the canvas. The flag affects painting alone, so ordinary and streaming views safely share cached geometry; source commits update geometry directly rather than interpolate glyphs.
- The font loads on a `core.Resource`'s goroutine, as parsing it takes about 120 ms on an M-series Mac; until then a formula is an empty em square. In WebAssembly the load takes about 0.7 s and still holds the page's one thread ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#formulas)).
- Assistive technology reads the canvas as an image labelled by Untie's structured description, spoken through `base/formula/locales` in English and Chinese, which `LabelFunc` follows as the source and the language change; the description's literal letters, digits and `\text` stay as written, and digits read as numbers. A text with hidden spans reads the label in the formula's place, never its TeX.
- `View.OnHit` maps hover, press and click points back into Untie's `Box.At` and `NearestCaret`, carrying the source part and overlapping `Diagnostics` so an editor can place a caret or route a diagnostic without rebuilding formula geometry.
- `RenderMath` gives `TextView` a `View` for each formula, `Display` for a block's; `TextView` places either as an attachment, whose span's `Content`, `$…$` or `$$…$$`, is what a copy takes ([TextView](textview.md#rendering)). `ui` drops `PartMathBlock`'s code type and padding for a typeset block, which takes the type around it as inline formulas do.

## Diagrams

`base/diagram` draws Mermaid diagrams with [limu](https://github.com/firede/limu), which reads every diagram type Pele, the Obsidian team's Mermaid renderer, draws (flowcharts, sequence, class, state and entity relationship diagrams, Gantt charts, pies, mind maps, timelines, sankeys and more) and lays each out as a scene: items in the order they draw, each with its outline's parts, its edges' markers, its label and its style. NSH has none, and limu's MyGo adapter, `limumygo`, is the reference its painter follows (MIT, `NOTICE`).

- One painter draws every type: an item's parts, each a path or a box with its corners rounded by the style's radius, filled and stroked as its `Paint` says; the markers at its ends; and its label, line by line about its `At`, turned by `Rotate` degrees. NSGo's canvas strokes, caps, joins and dashes as SVG does and strokes text, so a box is a rounded rectangle path, a line is stroked whole and a label's halo, which keeps it clear of the lines under it, is its text stroked first, where `limumygo` works around MyGo's painter.
- Labels are measured for limu's layout with the platform's text engine (`Scope.MeasureText`), in the fonts they are drawn in: the system font, bold and italic as the label's spans say, and the monospaced one for its monospaced spans. A layout is kept by its source and size, 256 of them for the process, so a list or a chat showing a diagram again lays it out once.
- The canvas is the scene's size in points, at `FontSize` (16) times the user's text size setting. Colors are limu's look resolved for each item from the theme: Pele's light theme (limu's `Default`) in light, and in dark its gray counterpart, the same hues lightened for a dark ground, following the appearance, as NSGo's own look is gray with a blue accent where limu's `KohuDark` is tinted teal. An app's `Theme` sets the colors it names over that theme, its chart series among them, and leaves the rest.
- Assistive technology reads the canvas as an image named by the diagram's `accTitle` and `accDescr`, or else as a diagram, through `base/diagram/locales`.
- `ThemeFunc` is `Theme`'s tracked form and reads its colors while recording the drawing. An app maps its scoped `ui.ColorsOf` palette into a `diagram.Theme` there, so palette and subtree appearance changes recolor the same canvas without rebuilding it or laying the scene out again. The gallery's document and chat renderers use this explicit mapping; `base/diagram` imports no `ui` or palette API.
- `RenderDiagram` gives `TextView` a `View` for each code block whose language is `mermaid`. `TextView` places it as an attachment centered in `PartDiagramBlock` around a sideways `PartDiagramBlockScroll`, its span's `Content` the block's fenced source, which a copy takes; a block still streaming behind the caret shows as code until it is complete, so a reply's diagram does not flash its parse errors.
- A diagram that does not parse, or that limu cannot lay out in finite numbers, shows its source as text in `ErrorClass` and `ErrorStyle`, the monospaced font in a `TextView`.

## Resizable panels

`ResizablePanelGroup` ports `gpui-base`'s sizing (`resizeState`, tested as NSH tests it), filling its container unless the app sizes it: a drag grows the panel before its handle by taking from the panels after it, nearest first, as far as their minimums allow, and shrinking below a minimum takes from the panels before.

- Panels are a slice, not children registering through a context as NSH's do: Go lists them statically, and `Visible` hides one. `Axis` is NSH's: `Horizontal` side by side, `Vertical` stacked.
- Each panel's flex basis, grow, shrink and limits come from the size it wants: what a drag gave it, else `Size`. Unsized panels share the rest with a basis of 0; when every panel has a size they grow in proportion. Minimums shrink by one ratio when the group is too short for them.
- A handle is a 3-point band, centered on its 1-point line, between two panels (`handleBandNode`) whose negative margins give its thickness back, so it takes no room yet lies over the boundary inside the group, above its neighbours: macOS gives a view its cursor and tracks the pointer only within what its parents show. NSH's band is 9 points; 3 keep the target on the line, as desktop split views do, instead of taking clicks 4 points into the panels beside it. Touch screens add a 10-point slop each side. `ResizeHandle` is the same band on a container's edge, its line on the edge.
- Drags resize on a state made from the panels' laid out sizes as they start, and only their end, or a screen reader's 10-point step, reports `OnResize`. A press on the band, which no pan took, is a drag that did not move, as NSH's handle reports one from the pointer going down: two in a row fit a `DataTable`'s column, and reach `ResizeHandle.OnDoublePress`, which NSH lacks, for an app to restore a default size.
- Each handle follows the pointer's progression itself (`ResizeHandleState`): hover makes it hovered, a touch going down pressed, its native pan dragging, the pan's or touch's end idle, since a drag leaves the band within a point. `RenderHandle` renders inside the line in a `Dynamic` of that state, so a drag draws it three times, and `ResizeHandle` reports it with `OnStateChange`.

## Dock

`dock.Area` ports NSH's dock in layers: the layout data (`dock/layout.go`: `dock.Node` trees, normalization to a fixpoint, edits, the app's layouts installed with their IDs repaired), `gpui-base`'s persisted format (`dock/state.go`, `DockLayout`'s JSON) and the component (`dock.go`).

- Container IDs come from a counter per area above the largest ID its layout holds, not one per process.
- Each container renders from a memo of its shape (kind and children's IDs), so selecting a tab or resizing rebuilds nothing; a split whose children change is rendered again.
- Each panel's content is a `core.Holder` the area keeps by key while its layout lists the panel, as NSH's render keys: the group showing the panel places it, so a panel moved to another group or dock, or across the compact presentation, keeps its state. A drawer dismissed at compact width leaves its panels built, out of the tree.
- A tab drag claims the touches with the responder (`OnMove`, after 4 points of a pointer or a long press of a finger), measures the groups' tab bars and content frames with `Ref.Bounds`, and resolves the target as NSH's: a slot between tabs, a content frame's middle to merge, its outer 35% to split. The indicator and the drag preview are overlays that never take the pointer.
- Zoom and the compact presentation narrow the tree to one group by hiding the containers off its path, so the panels keep their content.
- While `Compact` holds the docks are `sheetMotion` drawers and a bottom sheet, presented one at a time, without changing the layout's `Closed`. The app decides it, by its width or device; NSH's area turns compact below 600 points on its own.

| --- | --- | --- |
| `DockAreaApi` through a ref | `DockAreaRef`, bound as the area mounts | Go has no refs to components |
| `movePanel`'s `index` and `activate`, `null` for no group | `DockTarget.Before`, the tab it goes before, and `NoActivate`; 0 for no group | Go's zero value is the default: last, displayed |
| Nodes typed `split` or `tabs`, a region's `open` and `collapsible`, `panels` a list of options by `id` | One `dock.Node` struct with `Tabs`, `DockRegion.Closed` and `NoCollapse`, `Panels` a map of `NoClose`, `NoZoom` and `Hidden` by key; `nsgo/base` gives NSH's shapes | Go has no unions, and its zero value allows everything, as a panel NSH's `panels` leaves out does |
| `groupOf` and `zoomedGroup` give `null` for none | `GroupOf` and `ZoomedGroup` return an ID and whether there is one | Go's comma-ok result |
