# Testing

`nsgotest` runs an app in a Go test, headless by default and on a platform under `nsgo test -target`. It follows NSH's `nsh-testing` (`nsh/docs/dev/tooling.md`, "Headless runs" and "On a real platform"). Read this before changing `nsgotest`, `internal/testhost` or a backend's `testhost.go`.

## The model

Every batch goes to a model of the native views (`nsgotest/model.go`), which queries read and steps act on: frames, styles, props, texts with their spans, shadows and line limits, inputs, scroll offsets, the animated values gestures and scrolling drive (`Bound`, `AnimatedValue`), canvas nodes as child views, and what the app asked the platform for. Headless, the app runs on `core.ApproxTextEngine`, or the engine `TextEngine` gives, and a `core.ManualClock`; frames run only as the clock moves. Its platform plays audio as a test sets it where the environment says `Audio`: it records the sounds loaded and played (`Sounds`) and each player's commands (`Players`), decodes the durations `AudioDurations` gives, failing the rest with `Decode`, refuses plays with `NotAllowed` under `RefusePlayback`, and moves a player on only as the clock moves.

Mounted looks and bound animation values use owned `nsgotest.ViewStyle` and `nsgotest.AnimatedValues` records. Explicit boundary mappings preserve app-value identities for effects and custom cursors; geometry helpers and animated application delegate to the existing driver policies.

Mounted identity, accessibility and interaction settings use owned `nsgotest.ViewProps` records. Their label IDs, actions and ripple are copied when a mount batch arrives; policy helpers delegate to the private driver after explicit conversion.

`View.Kind` uses owned `nsgotest.ViewKind` constants mapped from the mount protocol. The model converts picker, camera, scan and save requests into the records in `nsgotest/file_requests.go`. Answer hooks receive those records too, through the test host on a target for photo picks, captures and code scans. A mounted web view’s settings and requests are recorded as `WebConfig` and `WebCommand`.

Selectors (`nsgotest/selector.go`) name what a user sees, as Playwright's: `id:`, `label:`, `text:` (a substring, or a field's text or its placeholder while empty; in quotes, `text:"Save"`, the whole text or a span's), `role:<role> <name>`, `view:<node>`, and a bare string as a test ID, else a label, else a whole text. `label:`, `text:` and a role's name take `/regexp/flags`.

A view shows when it has an area that nothing hides or clips: `Style.Clip`, a scroll view the user cannot scroll and the window clip; a scrollable view shows all its content after its start; modals show over the window, and header views and the keyboard's bar where the platform puts them. Matches come by layer, the topmost modal's first. `QueryMounted` and `FindMounted` match among every view mounted instead, shown or not, in tree order, for a test reading what the platform received of one. A view's `Bounds` is its frame in surface points, as `Ref.Bounds` reports it and `Press` aims at it.

Pointer coordinates, picking, visibility and scroll limits include each ancestor scroll view's content zoom, including fractional and nested scales. `Bounds` keeps the untransformed layout frame that `Ref.Bounds` reports; native reveal uses the zoomed viewport geometry instead and can move both axes of a zoomable scroll view. Reveal intersects nested scroll viewports and excludes an unrelated native view covering a viewport edge, such as a tab bar. It starts the scroll gesture in the visible remainder and moves only axes the scroll supports. Parent perspective applies after content zoom. NSGo models this explicitly rather than carrying NSH's unscaled scroll geometry into native target steps.

Headless runs use the phone idiom by default, as the root environment starts, whatever the project targets. `nsgotest.Idiom` sets the idiom alone over the default or `Environment` option's environment, in either order, so a test reaches what `Env.Desktop` gates, such as hover tooltips, without building a whole environment; under `-target` the platform's idiom stays, as `testhost.Pin` keeps it.

`Start` takes a root view and `StartApp` the app's function, as `nsgo.Run` takes either, and the app opens windows where `Run` would: on macOS, and on Windows and Linux for the app's function (a root view there fills the one surface, as `Run`'s does without automatic update checks). `StartApp`'s function runs once in the app's own scope, where its application menu, quit handlers and global shortcuts live; `Start` makes a root view the main view of a function of its own, as the macOS backend does. The main view shows in the main window, which `SetMainView` opens, where `CurrentWindow`, `MainWindow` and `OnClosing` work; the model shows that window and steps act on it. The app's own surface and the other windows run beside it: what they ask the platform for, such as alerts, permissions and context menus, is answered to the surface that asked, and macOS shows the application menu that the main window or the app's own surface sent last, none once that surface closes, as the core reads it for `YieldsToMenu`, while Windows and Linux show the main window's menu or the app's own when the main window sets none; their view trees, input, keyboard shortcuts, titles and window commands are recorded per surface. A window or a panel opened `Transparent`, and a window `SetWindowTransparent` changes, reports it in `Env.WindowTransparent`, as a compositing desktop does, also after `SetEnvironment` reports a new environment to every window. `SetWindowEnvironment` changes one window's environment apart from the others, the main window's for `""` or one the app opened by its title, as a platform reports one window minimized, zoomed, full screen or out of sight while the others show; the app's own surface, whose `Lifecycle` is every window's together, and the other windows keep the app's, and `SetEnvironment` sets every window's again. The options apply before the first render: `Clock` supplies the app's `ManualClock`, and `WallTime` sets its wall time (`ManualClock.SetWallTime`) after every other option, so the first render reads it; without them, the clock and its wall time start at 2026-01-01 UTC. `Measure` runs on the system's clock instead. Once the main window closes no view is left to act on, until the app shows its main view again.

| NSH | NSGo | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `text:` matches a whole text | In Go tests `text:` matches a substring, and `text:"…"`, a bare string and `/^…$/` match whole; TSX tests have NSH's | Playwright's `text=` matches substrings, and NSGo's Go tests already did |
| `role:button[Save]` | `role:button Save` in Go tests, a substring of the name unless quoted; TSX tests have NSH's | NSGo's form before the port; the name may hold spaces either way |
| Press takes only a selector | `PressWith` and `PressAtWith` take `core.Modifiers` for that press alone | Tests need Shift-click and other modified gestures through native input |
| Spans of a text are views of the model | `SpanStyle` and presses read a text's spans by their characters | Go tests name a text and its characters; no view per span is needed |

Widget timelines and Live Activities belong to the process, including calls made outside the view tree. The `Widgets` and `LiveActivities` options install a test backend for the selected services through `driver.WidgetTest` before the app starts and release it after the app closes. It rejects a second test app that asks to take the same process-wide backend. The recorder snapshots Go entries and activity content and uses the app's wall clock.

`nsgotest.WindowRequest` and `nsgotest.Share` record window and share requests as the model applies batches. A window request names its action independently of the protocol's action numbers, and a share keeps a snapshot of its files, text and anchor. Image, input, modal, screen, scroll, video and custom Picture in Picture settings and sticky-cell ranges use records maintained in `nsgotest`, keeping app assertions independent of the mount message definitions. The model copies image variant maps, scroll snap offsets and scroll-edge bar lists into those records.

## Windowless native drawings

On macOS, `PlatformTextEngine`, `RegisterFont` and `Render` expose native text measurement, process font registration and bitmap drawing through `nsgotest`, without starting a host. This is an NSGo addition beyond NSH's model: `nsgotest` may import the shared Apple player while `core` stays independent of backends. Rendering uses a private synchronous image resolver, never the main-thread canvas caches or dispatch queue, so it also works without an event loop. Each render releases its native images and bitmap and returns owned premultiplied RGBA pixels. It rejects unresolved animation slots and view paints, image errors, offscreen cycles and invalid or oversized dimensions. Windows, Linux and device services remain [roadmap](../roadmap.md#from-nsgos-issues) goals.

The headless software GPU model keeps devices per surface, selecting both the device and its error/readback tree by `GPUFrame.SurfaceID`; node IDs alone repeat across windows. Device loss and recovery use that window's state and pixel ratio. `Surface` exposes `GPUReadPixels`, `TriggerGPUDeviceLost` and `RestoreGPUDevice`, while `App.GPUFrames` records submissions across all windows. Frames for removed surfaces cannot reach another window's device.

## Steps

`Surface` delegates view queries, input, gestures, media reports, captures, sizing and waits to its window's model (`surface_steps.go`), using the original `App` algorithms. App-wide service fixtures and clocks remain on the original app.

`App.Post` queues app work until the next step or wait settles. `Surface.Post` uses the owner's queue too, checking the handle on the test goroutine before graph dispatch; a closed tree is refused even before its model's removal. On a target a fresh engine barrier and model drain first apply earlier callbacks' window closures. Headless the test owns the app's graph; on a target the callback is posted to the scope the native host rendered the app under, and the step's engine barrier waits for it. A step on a target is quiet only once no posts are queued after its last engine barrier, so a post that posts another settles in the same step. The test's separate graph still runs its own storage and model callbacks on the test goroutine, as `testhost.PostToTest` runs tsxtest's VM work.

Steps (`steps.go`, `pointer.go`) act as a user does and send what a platform sends, to the views that report it. A step whose selector matches no view, more than one it would act on, or a disabled one, fails the test. Before a step, alerts the last one left unanswered get their default answer: the cancel button, else dismissed when cancelable, else the step fails; `App.OnAlert` answers them instead.

- `Press`: a touch down and up when a view listens to touches, presses outside the overlays it is not in, then PressDown, PressUp and Press, on the match's middle (a text in a button is pressed on the text, also on targets); a canvas node presses the view under its middle, a native component's view takes the press itself on a target, as the system's switch does, and headless the system switch reports `change` with the value it flips to, which `Emit(selector, "change", value)` reports directly; a text's span takes `Index`. A touch-only listener or responder also takes a press without becoming a keyboard stop. Presses of one view within 500 ms of clock time count up in `Clicks`, and a press takes the keyboard focus off pressable views. `PressWith` and `PressAtWith` carry the requested modifiers on down, up and press, with zero modifiers on subsequent plain presses; the watchOS host rejects nonzero modifiers, and native context-menu conventions still apply, as Chrome on macOS turns Control-click into a context menu. `DoublePress` sends two presses in one step before waiting for animations. `PressAt` presses a point of the match, for a view that finds its own targets under the pointer, as a canvas or a flow diagram's layer does.
- `LongPress`, on a pressable view or a selectable text: PressDown, the view's `DelayLongPress` (500 ms) of clock time, LongPress and PressUp, a finger resting at least 500 ms, as a person's does. On a platform it rests 200 ms longer, past what a scroll view holds its content's touches back, and the test's clock goes meanwhile for holds timed on the engine, as a context menu's.
- `Type`, `Clear`, `Submit`, `KeyPress`: the field takes the focus, then a key and an edit per character within `MaxLength`; a line break in a single line is the return key, as `SubmitBehavior` resolves it.
- `Compose` and `Commit`: an input method composes in the field, which holds the values the app sets meanwhile, and commits, a `Type` and the composition's end. On a platform they go through its text input client as an input method's do: AppKit's and UIKit's marked text, Chrome's `Input.imeSetComposition`, GTK's preedit signal, IMM's composition string, which needs an IME on Windows and skips the test without one, and Android's `InputConnection`.
- `Drag`: a finger lands, rests 100 ms, moves 10 points a frame through its path and lifts, from the match's middle or, with `DragAt`, a point of it, such as a selection's handle; the press under it is cancelled once it moves 8 points. `Touch` puts finger 1 down for a test to `Move`, `Lift`, `LiftAt` or `Cancel` a step at a time, a frame apart.
- Past the slop the innermost pan a drag activates takes it, else the scroll view it runs along that can move that way, unless the touch responder blocked native drags.
- `Scroll`: drags a scroll view by a distance, 10 points a frame. `ScrollTo`, `DragScroll`, `Pan`, `Pinch` (about the view's middle) and `Rotate` report a platform's own gestures, and `Wheel` and `TouchpadScroll` a view's `OnWheel`, the latter as the platform's trackpad gesture on a target. A gesture's view is the one around the match that follows it, else the one under its middle. On targets, `Pan` drags from that view's surface center through offsets from the start. `Pinch` drives AppKit's magnification handler on macOS, UIKit's two-touch recognizer on the iOS simulator, Android's `ScaleGestureDetector` and browser touch input on the web. Physical iOS devices and other unsupported targets stop at the pinch step with a skip reason.
- `Hover` and `HoverOut`: the views from the match out with hover events replace those hovered before.
- `Report` takes an owned `nsgotest.Event` for a platform report no step makes, explicitly converting it to the private driver event. It copies URI, selection and touch slices, navigation state, and `nsgotest.TouchGeometry` maps and their position slices; `Data` retains the test's native payload. `NoCaretMoves` keeps typing from reporting the caret, as NSH's headless model types.
- `DropFiles`: the drag enters, then the files drop, on the match or the nearest view around it with `OnFileDrop`, within the modal it is in, as macOS's hit test finds it; none, or a disabled one, fails the step. `Navigate` and `PostMessage` report to a web view that need only be mounted, as a hidden one goes on loading.
- `CloseWindow` sends the main window's close button's request: its `OnClosing` handlers run newest first, and it reports whether the window closed. On Windows and Linux, a close that would end the app also asks its `OnQuitRequest` handlers. `RequestQuit` sends a user or forced `EventQuitRequest` to the app's own surface headless and on desktop targets, and counts the approval in `Quits`. A handler alone is tested by calling it and reading `WindowClosing.Canceled` or `QuitRequest.Canceled`.
- `Focus`: the text field the match is or holds takes the focus as a tap gives it; else, in a desktop or web environment or on those targets, the pressable or `Focusable` view the match is or is in, else the first inside it, is reached by pressing Tab from where the focus is, the stops on the way taking and losing the focus, so Return or Space then press it. A read-only or `NoFocus` match fails as a disabled one does, and so does a view other than a field elsewhere, or one Tab does not reach.
- `Key`: a keymap binding, `mod` being Command in Apple environments and Control elsewhere; else, in a desktop or web environment, Tab and Shift+Tab move the keyboard focus between editable fields and pressable views not `NoFocus`, in tree order; on desktops, the web, Android and iOS/tvOS, Return or Space press the focused pressable with their modifiers; else the focused field takes a character (shifted as the US layout does), Backspace or Return; else, in a macOS, Windows or Linux environment, the enabled application-menu item the key is the shortcut of is chosen, as `driver.MenuShortcut` finds it, which also decides when a `YieldsToMenu` binding yields: a role's own shortcut where it has none, but off macOS the editing roles', the first enabled item with the key taking it, except that on macOS a disabled item before it keeps the key from it, as AppKit stops at the first key equivalent that matches; a disabled item or submenu takes nothing; and while a field or web view has the focus a `NotInText` item takes nothing and keeps its key from the items after it, and in a Linux environment no item takes a key pressed without Control, Alt or a logo key; else Escape goes back. A key nothing takes does nothing, as on a device.
- `KeyDown` and `KeyUp` hold one key, never a chord, and let it come up, so a test drives `View.OnKeyUp` and `core.KeysDown` (#1046). `KeyDown` does what `Key` does as the key goes down; a modifier it holds joins every later `KeyDown`, `KeyUp` and `Key` until its `KeyUp`; a key already held goes down again as its auto-repeat; and `KeyUp` reports the up to the view its down went to, with the modifiers still held, failing the step for a key not held. `Key` lets its key come up too, so a full press fires `OnKeyUp`. Under `-target` a host holds keys through `testhost.KeyHoldSurface`, and `nsgotest`'s target keeps the keys it holds so a `KeyUp` of a key not held fails there as headless; a host without it, such as watchOS's, skips the test. Android's test agent holds native KeyEvents with modifiers and repeats, and waits for pending consume continuations before answering a key step. HarmonyOS drives the Go bridge and waits for its queue; native plain-view focus and native-action consumption remain unimplemented there.

`KeyCode`, `KeyCodeDown` and `KeyCodeUp` press a physical position (#1242). Under `-target` a host presses one through `testhost.KeyCodeSurface`, with the position from `driver.KeyPositionCodes` and the US layout's key there as the fallback name. macOS, Windows, Linux and the web name the key with the platform's current layout, as a real keyboard's key is named, so `KeyW` is `z` on AZERTY: macOS sends the AppKit key code with the characters the current input source types there and the side's device flag for a right-hand modifier; Windows sends the set-1 scan code and extended bit in `lParam`, with the virtual key the active layout maps the scan code to and the keypad's own virtual keys; Linux sends the XKB keycode as GTK's hardware keycode, with the keyval the active XKB group gives it and the keypad's and right-hand modifiers' keyvals; the web sends the DOM code and key location through the DevTools protocol, named by `navigator.keyboard.getLayoutMap`; the iOS simulator sends the HID usage, and a device's host reports the position with the key; Android's test agent sends the Linux scan code with the key code; and HarmonyOS reports InputKit's `KeyCode` for the position. A modifier held both by `KeyDown` of its name and by `KeyCodeDown` of a position stays down until both let it go, each holding its own side on Windows. `nsgotest`'s target keeps the positions it holds, releases them as the test ends, every other key, by name or by position, before any modifier, and skips a test on a host without the interface.

`Ref.Focus` commands follow the same platform stops in the headless model: Android and iOS/tvOS also focus enabled pressable and `Focusable` views for physical-keyboard activation, as desktop and web environments do. On native mobile targets the model waits for platform focus reports; it never grants focus from a mount command alone. A plain, hidden or `NoFocus` view or a view covered by a modal leaves the current focus alone. This differs from the `Focus` step, which reaches a stop as a user does.

Each step then takes frames while an animation that ends, a style transition whose `OnTransitionEnd` is pending, or a list binds the rows it paces ([lists](scrolling.md#lists)), for at most 2 seconds of clock time. Frames the app requests itself do not hold a step, since its own loops may never end; `ManualFrames` keeps steps from taking frames at all, for tests that look at animations on the way. `Advance` moves the clock a frame at a time; `FastForward` jumps it, running each due timer once. On a target the platform runs its own animations in real time while the core still times each `OnTransitionEnd` on the test's clock, so a step moves the clock only while the engine reports such a wait (`testhost.Options.TransitionEnds`).

Headless waits check the condition after settling and advance one frame between checks, as TSX's default waits do. The deadline uses real time, so an animation can start on a post from another goroutine any time during the wait. `ManualFrames` keeps the clock still during a wait. Waits on a target take a step's frames between checks.

Headless `nsgo test` adds `nsgocheck` to the effective build tags, preserving `GOFLAGS` and explicit `-- -tags` values. In such a build, each graph created with `RootOptions.CheckCommits` (every `nsgotest` app outside `Measure`, and the core's test graphs) records its creating goroutine and rejects access from another, while the graph is idle, with a `Scope.Post` diagnostic ([core](core.md)); an engine passes `engine.Options.CheckCommits` to its root. Target tests include the same diagnostics under `nsgotest`. Native `nsgodev` builds enable them; release builds contain no goroutine lookup or ownership branch; WebAssembly omits this goroutine-ID assertion in development and test builds because browser callbacks may resume on a different managed goroutine; commit-consistency checks still run. On native hosts, test graphs and their cleanup must run on the same goroutine.

## On a platform

CI runs the full Go gallery on macOS and the web in independent jobs, retaining JUnit results with skip reasons and screenshots. Tests that require iOS navigation, native context menus or local shell processes skip explicitly where those capabilities are absent.

The base part-style check and Android's source checks call `testhost.RequireSource` first. Native test hosts and browser library runs skip them because they carry no checkout (#530). On the host, the Android JVM fixtures compare animation, scroll snapping and projection, sticky offsets, and layout transitions against the Go driver. The Linux test job installs JDK 17 and sets `NSGO_REQUIRE_JDK=1` so a missing compiler or launcher fails the job. A local run without a JDK skips these fixtures. Android's native protocol and control tests run on the device.

The macOS backend's own Go tests (`internal/platform/macos`, `onMain`) run their closures on the original AppKit thread. A closure's `Fatal`, `FailNow` or `SkipNow` also exits the test goroutine, while the main thread keeps serving later tests; a panic is rethrown on the test goroutine with its originating main-thread stack printed.

`nsgo test -target <t>` builds the package's tests with the `nsgotest` tag as the target's package and runs each test in a process of its own (`cmd/nsgo/testtarget.go`). A package whose tests link no backend runs them headless in the target's process, giving the page its turn as each settles on the web, where the promise reactions of a TSX app wait for it. Tests and their host are discovered with the target's build tags, even when the package also has portable files, so target-only tests run and host-only tests are excluded. Each `Start` of a test opens another window on a desktop; a phone and a page show one app, so a test's second `Start` skips there. The test's `TestMain` calls `nsgotest.Main`, which gives the main thread to the backend's test host (`internal/testhost`) and runs the tests beside it, or `nsgotest.MainFunc` with a function that calls `m.Run` between the package's setup and teardown: the host runs that function in `m.Run`'s place and ends the process with its code only once it has returned, so the teardown, deferred calls included, runs once per test process. `TestTargetTeardownEndToEnd` (`cmd/nsgo`) runs `testdata/teardown` on the desktop it runs on and checks that each test's process tears down after the test and ends with the function's code; it opens windows, so it runs only with `NSGO_TARGET_E2E=1`. On harmonyos, whose module runs in ArkWeb, the test binary's arguments and environment travel in the HAP's `test.json` and its output returns through the page's console and hilog; the target carries a test host that opens an app in the page, presses the view the engine's frames put under a point, types into the focused field and scrolls by driving the page's `Scroller`, and takes a screenshot of the root `FrameNode` through `componentSnapshot`.

Android and an iPhone run a test only through the test host (`needsTestHost`). The tool gives the app launch a separate five-minute deadline and starts the test's `-timeout` at the host's start mark; a host that never starts reports a launch timeout. Android's runtime reads its protocol from the test process's standard output, and on both the tool reads a test's output and exit code between the start and exit marks the host writes with the run's token. So for `-target android` and `-target ios` the tool refuses, before it builds anything or looks for a device, a package whose tests do not link `github.com/nsgo-dev/nsgo` (a blank import in a test file is enough), or a backend that registers its test host itself as `internal/platform/android` does for its own tests (`hostBackends`), or declare no `TestMain` to start the host from (`testHostError`). A declared `TestMain` may still fail to call `nsgotest.Main`: Android's reader rejects unknown message kinds, payload lengths over 64 MiB and truncated frames, stops the process and sends a marked error for the tool to report even before the host's start mark, with a hint about stdout and `TestMain`. The simulators need no test host: a package whose `TestMain` starts it runs as the app, its output and exit code read between the same marks since `simctl launch` returns neither the app's status nor a fast app's output reliably, and any other package, a library's among them, runs headless through `simctl spawn`, which returns the process's own output and exit code.

`nsgotest.Start` then has the host open the app as its `Run` would, with the engine's `Observe`, `Event` and `Pin` options, the test's clock and a clipboard of its own: the model follows the batches the real engine sends and the scroll, focus and text events the platform reports, and the test's environment pins the appearance, locale, text size, reduced motion, increased contrast, the login launch reason, the application in front and whether the platform follows it, and, when set, the device name and model (`testhost.Pin`). The rest stays the target's own, its OS, idiom, safe area and system header heights among them, so an `Environment` with another OS cannot lay the app out as that OS inside the host backend; such a test runs headless or on that OS's target. Android, iOS, tvOS and watchOS also keep the device's screen size over the test's `Size`. Desktop app functions open their own windows on macOS, Windows and Linux (`testhost.WindowHost`), and the macOS host wraps a root view in an app function too. The engine reports each batch and event with its surface's number. The main window, the one `SetMainView` opens, keeps the `App` model and its ordinary steps. Each additional window has its own view, focus, edit and keyboard model. The headless app routes its services and clock through the original `App` while each window applies its own view changes. Native test hosts resolve the surface number to the same window for input, close requests and screenshots. Commands from the windowless surface still reach the test's app-wide model, and steps on any window settle all of them. The test's cleanup cancels a native context menu the app left open before disposing its graph, as the test's window stays open and the menu would otherwise keep tracking into the next test; on macOS and Windows it waits for the menu's tracking loop to end, and cancelling with none showing does nothing.

On iOS and tvOS, `testSurface.Resize` changes the test host's `UIWindow` frame and lays out its native hierarchy before settling the engine. Each test resets the window to its scene's coordinate-space bounds. This tests viewport layout, safe-area publication, capture and input, not a system Split View, Slide Over or Stage Manager arrangement; live automation rejects the step because its window follows the system. Android and watchOS still keep the device screen.

On macOS, the test host opens its windows with a subclass that passes AppKit's proposed frame through without fitting it to the screen. `NSInvocation` carries the rectangle across the Objective-C runtime's structure-return conventions on Apple silicon and Intel. The native capture tests check layout and PNG dimensions for `Size` and `Resize` on a window taller than the screen.

On macOS the test host accepts only its own pointer, keyboard and gesture events. A main-thread ring keeps the latest 64 scripted event types, window numbers and nanosecond timestamps (within one nanosecond after rounding), since AppKit may copy queued events. Window dispatch, local monitors and tracking callbacks reject other input before it changes the scene; AppKit's application events and the host's direct hover calls still pass. Only `host.Main` installs the filter, so ordinary apps and live automation keep their input. For a window that is not key, scripted keys run its filter, offer a Command or Control key to its views' and the menu bar's key equivalents, and otherwise reach its first responder directly; Tab enters AppKit's key view loop from the app's root, as the content view is outside it. Hover picks after layout or scrolling use the point of the last scripted pointer event or scroll, as posted events leave the physical mouse where it was. macOS, Windows and Linux count nearby successive scripted presses of the view the window's hit testing finds, using each platform's double-click interval, so a press of another view where the last one was, such as a page's button a press just pushed, starts a new sequence as headless instead of reaching the new view as the second click of a double click. The other native test hosts isolate physical input the same way while leaving ordinary apps and live automation unaffected: Windows drops physical mouse, pointer, touch, wheel, keyboard, gesture and context-menu messages in its message loop and window procedure outside scripted `testSurface` sends; Linux rejects physical GTK 4 pointer, touch, scroll, keyboard and gesture callbacks outside scripted `testSurface` steps, while trusted `Ref.SendInput` events pass by their XI2 XTest source and NSGo request serial, matched to retained GDK events before dispatch; iOS and tvOS install a `UIWindow.sendEvent:` filter from `host.Main` that admits only scripted simulator HID events (matched by sender ID or bounded timestamp history) and synchronous scripted steps; watchOS drops physical `hostTouch`, `hostText`, `hostAlert` and physical finger/Digital Crown `hostScroll` callbacks while allowing scripted steps and programmatic `ScrollTo` updates; and Android enables `TestInputGate` when launched with `TestAgent.EXTRA` so `NsgoActivity` and `NsgoView` ignore physical touch, key, hover, scroll, trackball and back input outside `TestAgent` steps. Web tests use a dedicated headless browser.

By default a test's window takes those keys without its app becoming active, but in an inactive app AppKit may give a click on a text field no focus, so a test that focuses a field would depend on which app is in front ([#346](https://github.com/nsgo-dev/nsgo/issues/346)). `nsgo test -target macos -activate` sets `NSGO_TEST_ACTIVATE=1` for the test process: before a field's focus click and before each key step, the surface activates the app (`activateIgnoringOtherApps:`) and waits up to 2 seconds, polling from the test goroutine, for it to be active with its window key, failing the step with both states when the system refuses, as a locked session does. Without the flag nothing activates, and a focus click that leaves the field unfocused while the app is inactive fails the step naming that cause and the flag (`testhost.KeyboardSurface`). The tool accepts the flag only with `-target macos`, and only for a package that links the native host and starts it from `TestMain`, as a library package's target run is headless. `TestMacOSActivationEndToEnd` (`cmd/nsgo`, `NSGO_TARGET_E2E=1`) runs `testdata/activation` both ways, and CI runs it on its macOS runner.

Steps resolve on the model, scroll the view into view a gesture at a time, and act through the host's native input; a step first waits for the engine to hand its pending commits to the platform, then ends once no batch came and nothing animated for 100 ms, checking the engine again before returning quiet. The model applies platform events and commits in arrival order, so a controlled field keeps the value the app commits after its edit, rejecting older values by the native edit count. The whole wait is bounded to 5 seconds; the next step reuses an unfinished engine barrier rather than spawning more waiters. `Start` first waits for the app's first batch, which comes late on Windows.

`Press`, `PressAt` and their modified forms can hit a field directly. Every target test host settles the focus from that hit or from a handler's `Ref.Focus()` by taking the platform's reported focus and blur events rather than applying focus requests in the model. Windows and Linux finish delivering the press on the window thread and include queued mounts in their quiet checks; macOS posts a queue marker after mouse up and checks for it together with queued mounts; the web finishes CDP mouse events before replying and mounts synchronously; iOS and tvOS track pending simulator HID releases through `NsgoWindow.sendEvent:` alongside queued mounts and layout motions; watchOS resolves tap focus before dispatching touch end and includes queued mounts in its quiet check; Android waits for runnables posted during `ACTION_UP` (such as `PerformClick`) before replying and includes the focused view in its quiet signature; and HarmonyOS pairs blur and focus reports without applying refused requests. A press that moves no focus uses the same quiet window.

Storage is a memory store, which the test reads through a graph of its own. Native tests give `Scope.DataDir`, `CacheDir` and `LogsDir` directories under the test's temporary directory, headless as on a target, so a test never writes to the user's. In a browser, headless library tests included, those paths are empty, as the web backend's are; the file-backed `MicrophoneInput` fake skips there because it needs real WAV files. `TestWebHeadlessLibrary` (`cmd/nsgo`) runs the headless model, `ui.Input` and TSX promise/animation tests in a browser and checks their JUnit results, including the recorder's explicit skip; the TSX cases keep browser-managed callbacks working in checked builds. Test builds have a directory of their own under `.nsgo/test/<target>`, removed once the runner closes, so a second run cannot replace what a live runner serves or executes; a later run removes a killed run's directory once it has gone a day without starting a test. `TestWebTestBuildsAreIsolated` checks two builds of one package with different tags: the first still runs its own code after the second builds, and closing it leaves the second runnable.

Physical iPhone test runs bridge app stderr into the marked console stream too, preserving panic diagnostics after the test host starts.

On a target, `App.CapturePNG` and `App.Capture` settle and return the platform's PNG or decoded `image.Image`; headless `Capture` skips because it has no native pixels. `App.Screenshot` sends the same PNG to the tool as base64 lines in chunks, each with the PNG's length and CRC-32, preserving relative subdirectories and spaces in its name; the tool fails the test when chunks are missing, repeated or inconsistent or do not add up to that length and CRC-32, as when a device's log drops or cuts lines, and saves complete images under `.nsgo/test/screenshots/<target>` or the `-screenshots` directory. It also fails the test of a screenshot whose path is absolute, has a parent part, was taken before under any spelling the volume treats as the same file, as `Home` and `home` on a volume that ignores case, or cannot be written, saving the test's other screenshots.

Alerts are answered at their buttons: an `NSAlert`'s, a `UIAlertController`'s label, the web's DOM button, Windows' drawn button, GTK's button, the `AlertDialog`'s. `ChooseMenu` and `ChoosePopup` choose the native menus' items: the main menu's on macOS, the menu bar's on Windows and Linux, and the context menu showing; headless, an item with a standard role and no `OnSelect`, chosen by `ChooseMenu` or its shortcut, does what the desktops do with it: Close runs the main window's close (`OnClosing`), Quit asks `OnQuitRequest`, Minimize, Zoom and Toggle Full Screen change the window state the environment reports, the editing roles act on the focused field, which also takes its own clipboard keys when no item has them as its shortcut, and any other role fails the step, while under `-target` such an item skips the test; phones and the web, which show no native menus, skip the test. `Advance` moves the test's clock a frame at a time, and after each step waits for the engine to run what the step's timers posted (`Surface.Sync`), so the timers they set, an interval's next run, fall due within the same advance; the app's frames follow the clock. What the test reports for a platform (`Load`, `Navigate`, `ImageSizes` and the like) and `ManualFrames` skip the test on a platform; `nsgotest.Target` names it, for tests that check only the headless model.

`SelectTab` resolves a mounted system tab bar and validates the selected index against its current items. Headless it dispatches the component's `change` event, even when the bar has no measured frame. The iOS test host sets the `UITabBar` item and calls the bar's delegate on the main thread. Android asks the test agent to click the native tab child on the UI thread. Both run the component's normal selection callback.

`Select` uses `testhost.TextSelectionSurface` on iOS, Android and HarmonyOS: UIKit's `UITextInput`, Android's `InputConnection`, and ArkUI's existing `ItemSelection`/`TextInputController` path. The range is UTF-16, ordered and clamped before native input; hosts read back the actual selection, keeping the model correct even without an application callback. iOS reports through its ordinary selection path after reading `selectedTextRange`, with duplicate delegate reports suppressed by `field.State`; Android's native callback runs on the UI thread. HarmonyOS waits for `onTextSelectionChange`, remembers that native range, and replaces it through the test typing bridge before placing the native caret. Desktops and the web retain their editing-key fallback. An unsupported target skips instead of changing only the model.

Each host holds a key as its platform does: macOS posts the key's `keyDown` `NSEvent`, marked as a repeat when its code is already held, or a modifier's `flagsChanged`, the modifiers held (`heldFlags`) joining every event after them; Windows keeps the keys held in the thread's keyboard state that `GetKeyState` reads, setting the previous-state bit of a key's repeat; Linux presses and releases through the window's key callbacks with GDK's state, pressing a held key again as its repeat; the web asks the tool for the browser's trusted `keyDown` and `keyUp` input, a held key's next down marked `autoRepeat`; and the iOS and tvOS simulators send HID keyboard events, of which UIKit reports a held key's down once. Desktop `Key` steps offer editing keys to the keymap before performing their editing action: Linux goes through its window controller, Windows its shortcut matcher before `editKey`, and macOS its event filter before direct Command editing actions; the web's captured `keydown` listener already sees them before the field. A consumed key submits, blurs and edits nothing. Linux releases every simulated key, including consumed ones, so a later press fires again. Headless `Key("mod+a")` selects the focused field's text when no binding or application-menu item takes it (#415, #767).

`Measure` runs the app as its users do, for the performance scenarios (`examples/gallery/perf_test.go`): on the system's clock, so `Advance` sleeps, with full-layout commit checks off for that app only, and with steps that wait quietFor for their events and then only for the platform to stop animating, looked at every 50 ms. Test hosts pass the check through `testhost.Options` and `engine.Options` to `RootOptions.CheckCommits`; measurements never change another test's checks. A ticking app never stops committing, so waiting for quiet held each step the whole 5 seconds, and asking the platform what animates walks its views: on the iOS simulator that polling was most of a ticking DataTable's 31% of a core, against the app's 6%.

The test agent's Java ships in every APK, but the runtime instantiates `TestAgent` from the launch intent only in a debuggable build — which `nsgo test -target android` (`nsgo.test`) and `nsgo dev -target android -automation` (`nsgo.automation`) make — so release and non-debuggable builds never create it and no other app sets the process's arguments or environment.

The Linux backend's own native tests (`internal/platform/linux`, `startTestWindow`) open their windows one after another on one locked OS thread, the one whose first window initialized GTK, as GTK may be called only there; a test's window has closed before the next test's opens, and a window still open after 10 seconds fails the next test rather than moving it to another thread.

`Surface.WindowState` observes desktop window state through the optional `testhost.WindowStateSurface`, synchronizing with the UI thread. macOS reads the NSWindow frame/visibility/key state, Windows reads the HWND frame/visibility/foreground state, and Linux reads GTK visibility/active state with X11 root coordinates where available; Wayland leaves `Frame.Move` false. This native-only observation skips headless tests and unsupported targets instead of fabricating state from mount commands. The `internal/testhost/testdata/windowstate` fixture covers visibility, non-activating panel placement and preservation, with a separate test requiring mixed-scale displays.

## Driving a running app

`nsgo dev -automation` links the agent (`nsgotest/automation.go`) into the app it runs, which answers the tool's requests with `App`'s steps on the running app ([tool](tool.md#automation)). It is DevTools' agent (`devtools.Automation`): DevTools' registry hands it each surface `core` registers, before the surface's first batch, and it follows every graph of the process (`devtools.Surface.Follow`), as a host embedding several views with `nsgo.NewView` has, hearing on each graph's goroutine each batch a surface commits and each event its platform dispatches until the surface unregisters. Surfaces are keyed by `(graph, surface ID)` internally and assigned unique monotonic automation window IDs (positive for every listed window, panel and embedded view, with `Window.Graph` identifying the owning engine, so `0` always means the default window in requests, while the first windowing app's own windowless surface keeps `0` for its alerts and any secondary graph's own surface gets a distinct ID). Each surface gets a model in target mode with no window of the test's: the agent feeds it as `startTarget`'s observer would, takes what came every 250 ms and before each request, waiting up to 10 seconds for a starting app's first window, as the app connects before it opens one, and gives it the backend's live surface (`testhost.Live(graph, surface)`) to act on. Steps' failures panic in a `testing.TB` of the agent's and come back as the request's error, as `tsxtest`'s `appTB` does.

- View pacing stays on, as the app's users have it, so a list mounts the rows near sight, and full-layout commit checks stay off for the app.
- Alerts stay pending for the tool to answer, and one the user or the app answered leaves the model with its `EventAlert`. Each surface numbers its alerts and menu items itself, so an alert is named by its automation window ID and alert ID when another surface's has the same ID, and `alert` lists the first windowing app's own as window 0 (secondary graphs' own surfaces get unique IDs).
- A batch a model cannot apply fails the request taking it and leaves only that surface's model lost, queueing nothing more, rather than ending the app, disturbing other engines, or answering later requests from a model half applied (`target.applyLive`).
- On macOS an app's own surface shows no view but holds the application menu and the alerts its function shows, so the agent models it too: `menu` looks in the window's menu, then that graph's own surface's, and chooses through the surface whose menu has the item, which fails while the menu bar shows another surface's; with `dock` it chooses in the app icon's menu through the surface that set it last, as the platform shows that one alone, whichever window the request names (and even after the last window closes); and the own surface's modal alerts are answered at their buttons or dismissed with `abortModal`.
- macOS, Windows, Linux, Android, the web, mini programs and Apple devices and simulators (iOS, tvOS and watchOS) have live surfaces so far: each backend's `testSurface` (`macos/testsurface.go`, `android/testsurface.go`, `windows/testsurface.go`, `linux/testsurface.go`, `web/testsurface.go`, `ios/testsurface.go`, `watchos/testsurface.go`) builds with `nsgoautomation` as well as `nsgotest`, and a mini program's `liveSurface` (`miniprogram/testsurface.go`, `automation.go`) with `nsgoautomation` on `js/wasm` and in host tests. On macOS, a live surface resolves each `(graph, surface)` across standalone windows, windowless own surfaces and mounted embedded views (`NewView`), and makes its window key without activating the app before keyboard input; on the web (`web/testsurface.go`, `web/automation.go`), it wraps the page's surface 0 and drives it through the `nsgoTest` CDP binding; on Windows, live surfaces wrap the running app's `*window`s (surface 0 for the root window, and opened windows and panels by surface ID) with posted Win32 messages and `PrintWindow` captures; on Android, a live surface wraps surface 0 and sends input steps over the pipe to `TestAgent.java`; on Linux, the backend registers each live window by surface ID and drives the same GTK input paths as a target test, including menu and alert actions and offscreen screenshots; on iOS and tvOS, live surfaces wrap each connected scene, use simulator IOHID/keyboard injection only on simulators while keeping the private IOKit HID and `UIKeyboardImpl` lookups strictly under `nsgotest || nsgoautomation`, and on physical iPhones and Apple TVs (or when simulator HID is unavailable) deliver presses, scrolls, drags, typing, keys and alerts through public UIKit hit testing, `accessibilityActivate`, `UIControl` actions and NSGo's event dispatch; on watchOS, a live surface wraps the watch's single surface on both physical Apple Watches and simulators through Go's hit testing and `opScrollTo` ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#automation)).

## TSX tests

A TSX app's `*.test.tsx` files import `nsgo/test` (`tsx/tsxtest`, NSH's `nsh/test`): `test`, `describe`, their `skip` and `only`, the hooks and `expect`, in a goja VM of their own with `atob` and `btoa`, and `open(entry, options)`, which starts the app with `nsgotest` and gives an `App` acting as a user does: `press`, `type`, `scroll`, `drag`, `dragTo`, `chooseMenuItem`, `waitFor`, `shows`, `texts`, `spanStyle`, the keyboard and the clock, and the app's services as `nsgotest` records and answers them: `storage`, `dialogs` and `onDialog`, `picks` and `onPick` (recording and answering `Picker.pickImages` and, with `camera: true`, `Picker.takePhoto`, where a thrown error rejects the photo), `menu`, `openedUrls`, `clipboard`, `requests`, and `speech`, `hearSpeech` and `endSpeech`. Its options give storage, a clipboard, a size, a safe area, an appearance, a locale, reduced motion, increased contrast, a platform, speech recognition, a camera, a clock, a host and a network. `nsgo test` adds a Go test calling `tsxtest.Run` to the package, so they run with its Go tests, headless or on a platform; `tsxtest.RunSteps` runs a test body of its own on an entry, which `nsgo test <entry>` reads from standard input. `nsgotest.Errors` takes what the app reports failing instead of failing the test, as a host's overlay shows it while the app runs on.

`text:` and a role's name (`role:button[Save]`) are whole texts, and a span's; `waitFor` holds once any view matches; keys are named as keymaps name them (`"escape"`, `"mod+k"`). A step returns a promise that resolves once the app is quiet and rejects with why it could not be taken (a disabled view: `is disabled`), or with what the app threw meanwhile, which `expect(() => step).rejects.toThrow()` reads: nsgotest's failures panic in the app's `testing.TB` (`appTB`) and the step recovers them. A failure the app reports outside a step fails the test at its end.

Without `clock`, the app's time passes as the test waits, a frame at a time, as it does on NSH's system clock; `clock: {}` keeps it still but for `wait` and `clock.runFor`, and `clock.time` and `clock.setSystemTime` set what `Date` reads (`ManualClock.SetWallTime`), moving no timer; Go code reading `Scope.WallTime`, as a `Calendar`'s today does, then reads it in the local zone too, as `Date` does. A Go test's `SetWallTime` keeps the location of the time it is given, and without it `WallTime` is the clock's own time, in UTC. `performance.now()` and frames' times count in binary fractions of a millisecond, so a second of 16.667 ms frames is exactly 1000.

The network answers by routes keyed `"<METHOD> <URL>"`, a `*` matching any text, through `tsx.WithFetcher`, and fails a request none lists; `"live"` is the real one. A route is a response or a function of the request: a request arrives on a goroutine of its own, so it posts the call to the test's goroutine, its VM's, also on a target (`testhost.PostToTest`), which runs it as a step or wait settles, and polls a promise it returns at each settle after.

`host` plays the embedding app: its params, and its handlers, a value or a function whose result, or the promise of it, answers; `app.host.send` sends the app an event, and `calls` and `posts` list what the app sent the names it handles. Without `host`, a headless run runs the page's host module nsgo.yaml's `web.host` names, as the page would: in a VM of its own with `console` and timers on the app's clock, its `createHost(player)` answering the app's calls and `player.hostEvent` sending it events, those sent before it starts reaching it as it does.
